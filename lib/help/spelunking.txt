Side-View Caves
===============

These caves are turn-based locations with their own movement rules.  A wrong
direction can step away from support and begin a real fall.  Read the stamina,
posture and Air display; animation never changes the resolved outcome.

The bottom status strip keeps HP, stamina, air and movement state separate
from messages. The cave section appears beneath them, followed by directional
climbing costs when relevant. These resources stay visible even with the
statistics sidebar hidden or while fishing.

Movement and safety
-------------------

Arrows or keypad    move, climb, descend, swim, or choose jump direction
Space / . / KP5     wait one turn
R                   rest on dry, stable ground; any key stops
[ / ]               list nearby creatures / items (no time passes)
H                   cycle or set an adjacent grip target (free)
J                   begin the multi-stage jump
Down after J        abandon the reach and drop straight down
Hold P + direction  peek one tile from a safe offset viewpoint
'                   hammer a piton at the current position
;                   deploy rope downward from the current piton
g                   pick up local objects
o                   open a nearby door or survey supply chest
c                   close an adjacent cave door
D                   disarm a discovered trap (d still drops an item)
T / Ctrl+T          tunnel (original / roguelike keyset), then choose direction
<                   use the exit under the player
Escape              open the normal in-game menu

Bare-rock gripping costs much more stamina than rope.  Stable ground allows
recovery by waiting; hanging from a piton does not.  A piton can support the
start of a jump, and a valid grip can arrest the rise or the held
ledge-reaching pause.
Diagonal grips and deliberate release are allowed.  Falls beyond a short safe
distance deal sharply increasing damage and may kill immediately.

You can step diagonally up from rope or a piton onto an adjoining supported
ledge without selecting a grip first. A diagonal step down can land on a
piton or catch a rope; without support, you fall. A suspended piton still
does not restore stamina.

Peek is not Look.  Hold P before pressing the direction: without P, that same
direction is a movement attempt.  Releasing P returns the viewpoint while
keeping legitimately seen cells.  Ordinary sight shows only the first part of
a drop; a downward diagonal Peek can reveal a long clear shaft and its enclosing
rock faces.  Visible passages retain their continuous floors and ceilings, but
rock still blocks creatures, objects and open space around corners.  Focus loss,
menus and Escape clear Peek.

Survey doors
------------

Occasional dry passages contain old survey doors. Stand beside one on stable
ground and use o to open or c to close it. A sole suitable adjacent door is
selected automatically; otherwise choose its direction. Each attempt is manual.
Locked store doors use your ordinary physical disarming skill. Opening, closing
or failing to pick a lock spends a normal turn; invalid attempts spend none.
There is no lock-picking XP, and a closed, unlocked door stays unlocked.

Closed doors block movement, sight and shots. Cave creatures cannot open them.
A creature or loose item in the doorway prevents closing it. Look distinguishes
open, closed and discovered locked doors; remembered doors show their last known
state. Mapping reveals doors but not their locks or hidden occupants.

Traps and survey chests
----------------------

New cave sections can contain a sparse spring-spike or stone-release trap
and an old supply chest on dry ground, away from the guaranteed route.
Searching happens as you move or wait: sufficient searching skill reveals a
nearby trap. Looking or peeking does not grant free searching attempts.
Trap-detection magic also reveals traps; object detection finds chests but
does not tell you whether they are trapped. Mapping alone reveals neither.

Use D to disarm a known trap. Stand beside it on stable ground, or on its
square. With more than one candidate, choose left/right or keypad 5 for the
current square. Each attempt is manual and costs one normal turn; failure
can spring the trap. Successful disarming awards 1 XP, once only. Blindness,
confusion and hallucination make disarming harder. These physical mechanisms
strike once; walking, landing or teleporting onto an armed floor trap fires it.
Spent traps are harmless and marked with a dim ^.

Use o beside or on a chest. Picking its lock uses ordinary disarming skill.
Opening a trapped chest springs the mechanism first; open it again to retrieve
the supplies if you survive. Disarming a chest trap does not pick its lock.
Supplies are placed on the ground for g to pick up. An empty chest is marked _;
it cannot be closed, reset or looted again. Existing saves retain their explored
sections unchanged; new fixtures appear only in newly generated sections.

Tunnelling and terrain
----------------------

Stand on a stable rock floor and use T (Ctrl+T with roguelike keys), then
choose any adjacent direction. Each attempt takes one turn and the ordinary
digging stamina cost, shown in Look and in the digging message. Attempts do
not automatically repeat. Your best carried digger is used temporarily unless
your weapon cannot be removed. Strength, digging skill and equipment matter:
shale is easier than limestone; iron-stained stone is hardest. An unsuitable
tool, exhaustion or an unsafe cut spends no turn or stamina.

A finished tunnel removes one rock cell, not the whole stratum. It immediately
updates sight and removes formations that lost their support. Cutting out your
floor can cause a damaging or fatal fall; loose items and ground creatures also
fall when their supporting rock is removed. An anchored rope can catch you if
you still have stamina. Tunnelling itself cannot be done while hanging.

Outer bedrock, pool-retaining rock, fixture foundations, a piton's last brace
and rock securing the established return route cannot be excavated. Cuts that
would leave loose loot underwater or occupants colliding are also refused.
There is no fluid-flow or whole-cavern collapse simulation. Very old authored
caves without a certified return route remain protected against excavation.

Standalone Stone to Mud and short wall-removal rays use the same safety rules
and remove the first exposed rock in range. Magic retains its normal costs;
a protected rock resists it. Earthquakes, destruction and chained wall-removal
effects remain unavailable here. Excavated terrain and fallen items persist
when you leave the section or save/reload; visited caves are never regenerated.

Shared equipment and survival
-----------------------------

Rest (R) accepts a turn count, ! for full HP or focus, * for full HP, focus,
stamina and held air, or & for those resources plus recovery from harmful
conditions. A class without focus uses HP for !. Rest does not refill scuba.
The ordinary world clock, hunger, healing, status decay and hazards continue.
Stamina recovers at the same rate as deliberate one-turn waiting, not twice.

Automated Rest needs dry, stable ground. It stops for sighted or detected
creatures, hunger, damage, unsafe footing or your input. It will not start on
a known armed trap or while magical travel is pending; wait manually instead.
Unseen, undetected traps and creatures are not revealed by the safety check.

The [ creature and ] item lists show only occupants in sight or detected in
this section. They distinguish the two and give above/below/left/right offsets,
not safe walking routes. Items in the same pile are listed separately, unknown
flavours stay unknown and ignored items are omitted. Arrows scroll, PgUp/PgDn
turn pages and Escape returns. These are read-only lists, not automatic travel
or pickup. While hallucinating you cannot distinguish nearby occupants.

Pickup and ignoring
-------------------

Stepping onto loose objects uses your ordinary automatic-pickup settings.
"Always pickup items" takes precedence; otherwise !g prevents pickup, =g
requests it, and =gN limits the number carried. "Always pickup items matching
inventory" also honours the first matching pack stack's inscriptions.
A full pack only takes what will fit in existing stacks; the rest stays here.
No items are taken from neighbouring tiles, and this does not add automatic
cave travel or fighting.

Use g for manual pickup, or Ctrl+G to apply automatic-pickup rules here.
Handling a batch of cave items takes one normal action, just like manual g;
automatic collection of gold alone takes no extra time. Hazards and creatures
continue during handling. Waiting or dropping does not trigger auto-pickup.
Chest contents stay on their square until you pick them up or step onto them.

Use k (Ctrl+D with roguelike keys), or Ignore in an item's inventory menu,
to ignore an individual item or an offered known category. Ignored items
disappear from the cave map, Look and nearby lists; they are not destroyed.
K (O with roguelike keys) shows them again so you can unignore or recover them.
Ignored carried gear is dropped here when safe. !d and !* protect against
auto-drop; equipped items need confirmation and stuck items cannot be removed.
If the cell cannot hold dropped objects, or cave storage is full, you keep them.

Dry shelves are home to chasm skitters, quick but fragile cave rats and slower,
hard-shelled stone beetles. These ground creatures cannot follow you across an
open shaft or through a pool. Look at a visible creature for its health and
description. Bump attacks need stable footing; fire, throw or use an adapted
spell when appropriate. Cleared cave sections do not replenish their creatures.

Cave creatures have explicit traits. All current kinds are animals; stone
beetles resist poison and cannot be confused. Effective weapon brands and
slays work in melee and ranged attacks. Sleep, hold, slow, haste, confusion,
stun and fear now affect cave creatures; visible Look and creature lists
show their current conditions. Damage wakes a sleeping creature.

Creature teleportation needs an empty dry shelf. Polymorph changes creatures
within the cave roster, not into an arbitrary dungeon monster. Supported
summons are hostile, need nearby space, and give no kill XP. Polymorph cannot
increase a creature's original XP award. These conditions survive saving.
Probing and sight-wide magic require sight; detection alone does not let a
spell hit through rock. Banishment gives no XP and still strains your health.

Cave Survey (Shift+C, then the Cave Survey tab)
  Left/Right changes character tabs. Press Enter, or click a section, to
  explore the survey. All four arrows now select sections in the diagram;
  Tab / Shift+Tab cycles through every known section, and Home selects here.
  Escape (or right click) returns to the tabs; Escape again closes the screen.
  The @ marks your actual location, not the highlighted selection. Inspecting
  a section never moves you or spends a turn. The diagram shows connections
  and survey depths, not compass directions or exact entrance positions.
  Only discovered sections/passages appear. Section numbers are references
  within the current chart; the chart may rescale/renumber as you discover more.

Spellbooks must be carried.  Studying remains possible for every normal spell,
including one whose effect only works outside a side-view cave.  Casting keeps
the normal focus cost, failure chance and turn cost.  Aimed spells use the
familiar eight directions; they collide with cave rock and creatures.  Effects
without side-view meaning are refused before focus or a turn is spent.

Scuba gear supplies a visible reserve underwater.  When it is exhausted,
ordinary Breath drains until you surface.  Surface recovery does not recharge
the gear.

Commands which still need side-view spatial rules are blocked rather than
silently using a top-down target.  These include conventional targeting,
the ordinary dungeon-map view and automatic cave navigation.
Cave Survey and cave-native Look are
the deliberate side-view alternatives to the two map screens.
